# Big Tech Keeps Buying Chips It Cannot Plug In

## Chip depreciation and the standoff

Nvidia rolls out a new chip every 18 to 24 months, and each generation makes the last one obsolete, slashing its resale value in half. By the end of the decade a $30,000 chip bought in 2024 will be worth about as much as a used car engine block, and data centers are full of them — billions of dollars of silicon on pallets in a dark warehouse, slowly bleeding value while waiting for a substation that is not coming. Every CEO in Silicon Valley already knows this and orders more chips anyway: if Amazon stops buying while Google's dark buildings light up the next day, Amazon never catches up. It is a Mexican standoff in which each company is terrified of blinking. Nvidia, the ultimate arms dealer in that standoff, gets paid the moment the shipping crates leave the warehouse, and a single rack of its latest chips pulls as much power as about 100 American homes — whether the grid survives is somebody else's problem.

## Meta and Microsoft double down

Mark Zuckerberg proved he would rather burn billions than come second in the AI arms race: Meta's 2026 spending was pushed to $145 billion, and investors, unable to see how Meta would ever earn it back, dumped the stock — $175 billion wiped out in a single day. Zuckerberg did not blink; three weeks later he slashed 8,000 jobs to keep the spending alive. Over at Microsoft, CEO Satya Nadella gambled even bigger: a record $37.5 billion into AI and data centers in a single quarter, more than $400 million every day, with finance chief Amy Hood warning that AI demand was rapidly outpacing supply. Investors turned their backs and $357 billion in value was wiped out by the end of the next day. Nadella has admitted Microsoft has chips sitting in storage with nowhere to plug them in — yet the company signed $129 billion in leases for data centers that have not even opened, some locked in for 20 years. If the AI boom goes bust tomorrow, Microsoft still has to pay every cent.
