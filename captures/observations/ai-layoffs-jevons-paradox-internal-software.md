# AI Layoffs and the Jevons Paradox of Internal Software

Cloudflare's CEO compared laid-off workers to manual screwdrivers while admitting AI eliminates the need for redundant engineering "benches." The hidden opportunity is the long tail of internal software that was never economically viable until now.

## What the CEO said

When announcing layoffs, the framing was deliberately blunt: the laid-off engineers are like the manual screwdriver — useful once, then displaced. The new world runs on a different tool (AI), and bench engineers whose job was to absorb overflow or staff projects AI can now do directly are not needed in the same shape.

The phrase that stuck: **engineering bench**. It names a specific role: a senior engineer kept on the team not for a project they are running, but for the projects that show up. They are a buffer, a queue absorber, a "we have someone who can pick this up" assurance.

## What the layoffs reveal

Three things, in order of how much they matter:

1. **The bench role was always a tax.** Companies kept the bench because the alternative — projects sitting unstarted, executives frustrated — was worse. AI collapses the alternative. A 5-person team with AI can do the work a 15-person team used to do, and the 10 extra people were not adding 10x of output; they were absorbing variance.
2. **The CEO is right about the tool change and wrong about the ceiling.** AI raises the floor. The ceiling is still set by the product sense, the design taste, the system architecture, and the people who own those things. Companies that fire the bench and keep the people who decide what to build will out-execute. Companies that fire everyone will discover that the tool does not know what to build.
3. **The internal-software tail just got longer.** The under-discussed part: a lot of internal software was never built because the bench engineers were already busy on the visible product. The visible product is now cheaper. The bench hours are now free. The internal software backlog — the tools, the dashboards, the glue — finally has a budget.

## The Jevons paradox applied to labor

Jevons paradox, classically: a more efficient engine consumes more coal, not less, because cheaper steam power opens up new uses that were not economic at the old price.

Here: cheaper engineer-hours (because AI produces more per engineer) does not produce less engineering work. It produces more, because the new price point makes the long tail of internal-software work viable. Companies that fire the bench and pocket the savings will underperform companies that fire the bench and invest the savings into the internal backlog.

The opportunity is not "we can do the same work with fewer people." It is "we can do a category of work we were never doing, because it was never affordable, and now it is."

## What this means in practice

- For laid-off engineers: the relevant skill is not "I am a senior engineer." It is "I know which internal-tool problems are worth solving and which are not." That taste was the actual scarce resource the bench role was providing.
- For the companies doing the laying off: the win is in the projects you green-light next quarter, not in the savings line. If the savings line is the only thing that grows, the bet is wrong.
- For everyone watching: the "AI replaces engineers" story is a transitional story. The "AI expands the surface area of what gets built" story is the steady-state one. The companies that internalize the second story will compound.
