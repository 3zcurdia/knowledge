# AI Data Centers Are Hitting the Grid's Limits

## Interconnection queues

Ashburn, Virginia — ground zero for the AI buildout — packs nearly 50 million square feet of data center space into one county, and over 70% of the world's internet traffic flows through it daily. The facilities look like thriving hubs but sit hollow: AI companies are held hostage by local power company Dominion Energy, whose grid is paralyzed. The wait to bring a new data center online was four years in 2022 and had stretched far beyond that by 2026; tech giants like Microsoft are quoted up to 12 years just to have a connection request evaluated — not to lay copper or build a substation, only to decide whether it is even possible. Dominion's grid has a 25 gigawatt ceiling, enough to power millions of homes, while queued data centers demand 40 gigawatts — the output of 30 nuclear reactors for a small corner of Virginia. Across the country, $130 billion of real estate sits in limbo, and there is no cutting in line: even a billion-dollar Amazon or Google data center joins the same waiting list as a local substation.

## Workarounds and constraints

Desperate to get power, AI companies are skipping the grid by building their own gas plants on site. Each plant needs a giant gas turbine, and GE Vernova — America's biggest turbine maker — has a waiting list running through 2030; every turbine a tech giant secures leaves Texas more exposed to the next blackout. Taxpayers funded new power plants as a public safety net, but eight of the state's chosen energy projects walked away after being squeezed out by AI demand. Even with its own plant, a data center stays dark until electricians wire it in, and America faces a catastrophic trade shortage: almost 20,000 electricians leave the industry every year, three in 10 of those who stay are over 50, and data-center electricians can earn up to $260,000 a year — easily out-earning a software engineer. In Abilene, Texas, OpenAI's $500 billion Stargate project pays electricians almost double what local homebuilders can offer, with 100 more data centers headed for Texas. Energy demand is exploding: AI projects waiting to be plugged in in Texas demand 474 gigawatts, more than five times the record peak of the entire Texas grid, and data centers make up almost all of those requests. In August 2026 Governor Greg Abbott ordered an emergency freeze on new data centers; ERCOT warned that without a full grid overhaul the Permian Basin — America's biggest oil field — could face rolling blackouts just to keep up with AI demand.

## Who pays for the capacity

PJM, the largest grid in America, stretches across 13 states and Washington DC with Ashburn wedged into its heart. Every year PJM runs an auction that pays power plants just to stand by for sudden surges, and every household on the grid covers that cost through its monthly bill. AI companies forced their way in and began devouring every spare megawatt; in one auction the price shot up more than six times. Utilities are passing those costs onto customers: households across the region are paying an estimated $25 to $30 more a month than in 2024, according to research firm Semi Analysis. Joseph Bowring, the independent market monitor who polices PJM's market, traced the new costs straight to Big Tech — data centers are the primary reason for the soaring prices, he wrote, having already added $29.4 billion to customer costs, damage he considers not reversible. The White House has demanded new power plants be built and brought online as soon as possible with AI companies paying for them; one governor threatened to pull his entire state out of PJM if the utility company did not act.
