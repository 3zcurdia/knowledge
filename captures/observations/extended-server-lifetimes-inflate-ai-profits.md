# Extending Server Lifetimes Flatters AI Profits

## The filing change

When Nvidia shipped its next-generation Vera Rubin chip in the summer of 2026, the chips sitting in warehouses across America fell a full generation behind overnight. Microsoft and Google could not let Wall Street see the bleeding, so they altered a line in their financial filings: the assumed life of their AI servers was extended from four years to six. With the stroke of a pen, billions in costs were pushed further down the road — overnight, operating expenses disappeared and profits artificially swelled. Meta pulled that exact same stunt, instantly padding its profits by $2.6 billion without selling a single extra ad; it had already stretched its servers' lives twice before, padding profits by more than $5 billion.

## Reversals and pushback

In 2025 Meta suddenly reversed course, cutting some servers back to five years and admitting the reason: artificial intelligence is making hardware obsolete faster than expected. Coming clean and telling the truth cost Amazon about a billion dollars in profit. Michael Burry estimates that Big Tech will hide $167 billion in costs using this trick, calling it one of the more common frauds of the modern era. Nvidia immediately fired back with a defensive seven-page memo to Wall Street analysts, desperate to claim that its chips stay useful for up to six years. Burry is not backing down: he is now warning that Oracle's profits will soon be overstated by more than a quarter and Meta's by about a fifth.
