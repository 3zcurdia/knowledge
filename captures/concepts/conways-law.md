# Conway's Law

Organizations which design systems are constrained to produce designs which are copies of the communication structures of these organizations. Named after Melvin Conway (1967).

## Core Idea

The technical structure of a system reflects the social boundaries of the organizations that produced it, across which communication is more difficult. In short: complex products end up "shaped like" the organizational structure they were designed in.

## Interpretations

The law only describes correspondence, not causality. Different positions exist: that organizational structure dictates technical design, that technical design causes organization to restructure, or both.

## Variations

- Yourdon & Constantine (1979): "The structure of any system designed by an organization is isomorphic to the structure of the organization."
- Coplien & Harrison (2004): "Make sure the organization is compatible with the product architecture."

## Evidence

MIT and Harvard researchers found "strong evidence to support the mirroring hypothesis" — loosely-coupled organizations produce significantly more modular products than tightly-coupled ones.
