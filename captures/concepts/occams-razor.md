# Occam's razor

Occam's razor (also Ockham's razor or the **principle of parsimony**) is a problem-solving principle that recommends searching for explanations constructed with the smallest possible set of elements. Attributed to William of Ockham, a 14th-century English philosopher and theologian, it is frequently cited as *Entia non sunt multiplicanda praeter necessitatem* — "Entities must not be multiplied beyond necessity."

When presented with competing hypotheses about the same prediction and both hypotheses have equal explanatory power, prefer the hypothesis that requires the fewest assumptions.

## Key formulations

- **Original**: *Pluralitas non est ponenda sine necessitate* — "Plurality must never be posited without necessity"
- **Newton**: "We are to admit no more causes of natural things than such as are both true and sufficient to explain their appearances"
- **Einstein**: "Everything should be kept as simple as possible, but not simpler"
- **Russell**: "Whenever possible, substitute constructions out of known entities for inferences to unknown entities"

## Applications

- **Science**: Used as a heuristic to guide theoretical model development, not as a rigorous arbiter. Prefers simpler theories because they tend to be more testable and falsifiable.
- **Medicine**: The "Zebra" rule — reject exotic diagnoses when a commonplace explanation is more likely.
- **Software**: Rule of least power — use the simplest programming language that solves the problem.
- **Statistics**: Bayesian model comparison uses Occam factors to balance complexity and power. Formalized via AIC, BIC, minimum description length, and Solomonoff's theory of universal inductive inference.

## Mathematical basis

All assumptions introduce possibilities for error; if an assumption does not improve accuracy, its only effect is to increase the probability that the overall theory is wrong. A study of 97 economic forecasts found complexity increased forecast errors by an average of 27%.

## Caveats

- Not an embargo against complex explanations — only adjudicates between theories with equal explanatory power and equal evidential support.
- Simplicity can mean structural (syntax) or ontological (semantics) — these often pull in different directions.
- Francis Crick warned: "While Ockham's razor is a useful tool in the physical sciences, it can be a very dangerous implement in biology."
- The no-free-lunch theorems show Occam's razor must rely on assumptions about the prior probability distribution of our world.
