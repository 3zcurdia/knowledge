# Breakfast Sausage Patties: Sage, Fennel, Maple & Cayenne Pork

Loose, uncased breakfast sausage for a New York–style bodega sandwich: 450 g (1 lb) ground pork seasoned with fresh sage, fennel seed, garlic, maple syrup, Diamond Crystal kosher salt, cayenne and finely chopped bacon, hand-mixed until the mixture turns sticky and a film develops on the bowl, then test-patted, seasoned to taste, and formed into 3–4 oz patties pressed flat on a griddle until caramelized.

**Yield:** 4 sausage patties (3–4 oz / 84–112 g each) from 1 lb of ground pork

**Cook:** Not stated in the video — cook on a hot griddle until browned around the edges and cooked through

## Ingredients

- 450 g (1 lb) ground pork
- 113 g (4 oz, about 4 slices) bacon, finely chopped
- 30 ml (2 tbsp) fresh sage leaves, finely chopped
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) maple syrup
- 5 ml (1 tsp) fennel seed, crushed
- 5 ml (1 tsp) Diamond Crystal kosher salt, per 450 g of meat
- 1.25 ml (¼ tsp) cayenne pepper
- 1 clove garlic, finely grated
- Freshly ground black pepper, to taste

*Quantities are from the video description; in the video the salt is given only as a rule of thumb — a pound of meat to a teaspoon of kosher salt, at least if you are using Diamond Crystal kosher. Sage is measured by volume (about 2 tbsp), and the patties are formed at 3–4 oz each.*

## Steps

1. **Chop the sage** — Pick the leaves off the fresh sage and chop them finely; you need about 30 ml (2 tbsp). The leaves are furry and tough, so chop them small. Taste as you go: if you are not sure you want a very sage-forward sausage, hold a little back and add it later.
2. **Crush the fennel seed** — Crush 5 ml (1 tsp) of fennel seed on the cutting board with the side of the knife — more crushing than chopping. Keep your other hand curled over the seeds so they do not fly, and do not lift the knife high. It does not need to be uniform, just broken up so it distributes through the sausage. (The video description says to crush it under a heavy saucepan.)
3. **Grate the garlic** — Finely grate 1 clove of garlic.
4. **Add the cayenne** — Add ¼ tsp (about two small pinches) of cayenne for warmth, not heat; the heat brings out the other flavors and plays against the maple sweetness.
5. **Add the maple syrup** — Add 1 tbsp (15 ml) maple syrup. The sweetness is essential to breakfast sausage, and the sugar also gives the patties excellent browning.
6. **Add the salt** — Add 1 tsp (5 ml) Diamond Crystal kosher salt per 450 g (1 lb) of meat. Kosher salt needs more than table salt and sometimes needs a bit more, but the bacon brings its own saltiness.
7. **Freeze and chop the bacon** — Put the bacon in the freezer for about 15 minutes, just until it is firm (not frozen solid), then chop it as finely as possible and go back over it to catch any chunks. Prefer the fattier slices from the pack; perfectly uniform bits are not required.
8. **Combine** — Add the chopped bacon to the ground pork in the bowl.
9. **Mix by hand** — Wash your hands and mix with a kneading motion: fold the mixture back onto itself, press, and rotate the bowl. Keep mixing and folding until the mixture pushes back against your hands with a bounciness, a film develops around the side of the bowl, it feels sticky and firm, and it holds together as a single mass with more resistance when pressed. Err on the side of under-mixing — you can always mix more, but you cannot undo overworking.
10. **Make a test patty** — Pinch off about 1 tbsp of the mixture and flatten it into a little patty.
11. **Cook the test patty** — Preheat a carbon steel skillet for about 45 seconds and cook the test patty. No fat needs to be added to the pan — there is plenty of fat in the sausage and some of it renders out. You do not need high heat, just to cook it through; the maple sugar gives good browning, so you do not need to sear it. It is mostly cooked after the first side.
12. **Taste and adjust** — Check three things: the seasoning (is the salt right, too much or too little?), the overall flavor profile (do you want more maple or extra sweetness, how is the sage, how is the garlic), and the texture (a bouncy sausage-meat texture that holds together with good cohesion). If it falls apart it needs a little more working; if it is overworked considerably it will turn into crumbles.
13. **Form the patties** — Divide the mixture into 3–4 oz (84–112 g) patties and stack them between layers of parchment paper. The patties freeze well — wrap them individually and freeze the ones you are not cooking.
14. **Press and cook** — Press the patties flat on a hot griddle with a press (a sheet of parchment between the patty and the press, or a little oil on the press, stops them sticking) so they do not shrink and stay the same size as the English muffins. Cook until they start to brown around the edges with good caramelization, then move them to a sheet tray.

## Notes & tips

- No grinder and no casing: this is loose sausage meat — seasoned pork formed into patties — so it is easy enough to mix by hand, and a pound is a very makeable quantity.
- Salt rule of thumb: 1 tsp (5 ml) Diamond Crystal kosher salt per pound of meat; the bacon adds more saltiness on top.
- Do not over-mix. Under-mixing is recoverable — you can mix more after the test patty — but overworking turns the mixture into crumbles.
- Freezing: make a pound, wrap the formed patties individually and freeze them; cook them from frozen or thawed.
- Maple sugar is the browning engine: it gives the patties great caramelization even without a hard sear.
- Bacon prep detail: chilling it ~15 minutes firms the fat so it chops fine; the size of the bits need not be uniform, but go back over it with the knife.
- The recipe was originally written for the book *What's for Dessert* but was cut by the editor, so it is not in the book. It was meant to sit alongside the English muffin recipe.
- Equipment: the main need is a large griddle (carbon steel) or a large skillet; a press gives very even, flat patties.
- Claire grew up in St. Louis, a barbecue town — specifically St. Louis style ribs — and would have grown up eating pork.
