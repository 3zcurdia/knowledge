# Homemade Pastrami: Cure, Smoke, and Rest

Homemade pastrami built from a 14 lb (6.4 kg) choice-grade brisket: trim, cure 7 days in a pickling-spice brine, smoke at 250 °F (121 °C), then rest until probe-tender.

**Cook:** ~10 hours (5 hours unwrapped + 5 hours foil-wrapped) plus at least 2 hours resting

## Ingredients

### Pickling spice

- 15 ml (1 tbsp) coriander seeds
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) black peppercorns
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) mustard seeds
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) red chile flakes
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) allspice berries
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) whole cloves
- 5 ml (1 tsp) ground ginger
- 5 ml (1 tsp) ground mace
- 1 small cinnamon stick
- 2 bay leaves

### Brine

- 3.8 l (1 gallon) water
- 360 ml (1 1/2 cups) kosher salt
- 240 ml (1 cup) granulated sugar
- 20 ml (4 tsp) pink curing salt
- 10 garlic cloves, crushed
- Whole pickling spice (all of the blend above)
- About 3.8 l (1 gallon) ice

### Meat & rub

- 6.4 kg brisket (14 lb), choice grade
- Rub: black pepper, coriander (coarsely ground), garlic powder
- Yellow mustard (binder)

*Exact rub quantities are not stated in the video source — do not guess them.*

## Steps

1. **Trim the brisket** — Remove almost all surface fat from the point (it already has plenty of intramuscular fat); leave at least 1/4 inch (6 mm) of fat on the flat, which tends to dry out.
2. **Toast and grind the spices** — Toast black pepper, coriander and mustard seeds in a dry pan, keeping it moving so it doesn't burn; a few minutes, until fragrant. Grind coarse with a mortar and pestle, then mix with the 1 tbsp whole cloves, red chile flakes, allspice berries, 1 tsp ground ginger, 1 tsp ground mace, 2 crushed bay leaves and the crushed cinnamon stick.
3. **Boil the brine** — In a large pot, combine 3.8 l (1 gallon) water, the whole pickling spice, 360 ml (1 1/2 cups) kosher salt, 240 ml (1 cup) granulated sugar, 20 ml (4 tsp) pink curing salt and 10 crushed garlic cloves; mix and bring to a boil, then remove from heat immediately.
4. **Cool the brine fully** — Set a cooling rack in a large container and add about 3.8 l (1 gallon) of ice; the ice both chills the brine and provides the second gallon of water (2 gallons / 7.6 l total). The brine must be 100% cool before the meat goes in — you do not want to cook the brisket.
5. **Brine 7 days** — Submerge the brisket; it will float, so weight it down. Seal the container tightly with cling film and refrigerate 7 days.
6. **Wash off the salt** — After 7 days, remove the brisket and wash the salt off completely ("wash it like you mean it"); the salt has penetrated over 7 days, and skipping this step makes it extremely salty. Dry thoroughly.
7. **Season with the rub** — Lightly coat with yellow mustard so the rub sticks, then apply the coarse pepper-coriander mixture with garlic powder. The brisket shrinks as it cooks, so season accordingly and don't over-season.
8. **Smoke at 250 °F (121 °C)** — Smoke 5 hours, until a deep red mahogany color and the desired amount of smoke.
9. **Wrap and finish cooking** — Wrap in aluminum foil and return to the cooker for about 5 more hours at 350 °F (176 °C); go by feel, not the clock — it's done when a probe inserts with no resistance (probe-tender).
10. **Rest 2+ hours** — Wrap in a towel and rest in a cooler for at least 2 hours.
11. **Slice and serve** — Slice and check doneness with a bend test: the meat should flex and pull apart tenderly. Optionally build a Reuben: toast bread in butter until golden brown, then layer Thousand Island dressing, pastrami, sauerkraut and melted baby Swiss.

## Notes & tips

- Fat trimming: leave at least 1/4 inch (6 mm) on the flat; remove nearly all fat from the point.
- Do not skip the post-brine wash — without it the pastrami will be extremely salty.
- The brisket shrinks during cooking; watch the seasoning so the crust doesn't become oversalted.
- Red mahogany color after 5 hours signals good smoke penetration; that's the cue to wrap.
- Judge the wrapped phase by probe feel (no resistance), not by time.
- The video does not state exact rub amounts — only the 1 tbsp of whole cloves and 2 bay leaves in the pickling spice are quantified in the transcript.
- A small patch of the brisket may not pick up the cure; trim it off if needed.
