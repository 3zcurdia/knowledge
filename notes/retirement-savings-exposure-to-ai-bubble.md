# Retirement Savings Are Exposed to the AI Bubble

## Concentrated exposure

Big Tech stock is buried in the 401(k)s of 70 million Americans — $10.8 trillion, more than a third of the U.S. economy. Out of every $100 in a standard S&P 500 fund, about $34 rides on just seven tech giants, Nvidia the largest of them all; if they fall, those accounts fall with them. On June 5, 2026 the market got its first warning shot: in a single trading session chip makers shed over $1 trillion, with Nvidia alone losing about $300 billion in hours, and every retirement fund holding those stocks got hit. That was just one day — when the .com bubble burst, the NASDAQ kept sliding for more than two years, losing 78% of its value, and it took 15 years to climb back.

## Washington is pushing workers deeper

The White House has ordered regulators to open 401(k)s to private credit markets — the same toxic asset pool that Blue Owl shut to its own investors when they tried to withdraw. A new Labor Department rule would strip away workers' rights, making it harder for them to sue if these investments go bad. The shift is already happening: one of the largest 401(k) providers in America has partnered with Apollo and PIMCO — the firm holding billions in Hyperion bonds — and more than 200 employers have already signed up.

## Insiders are cashing out

Over the summer of 2025 Amazon founder Jeff Bezos unloaded $5.7 billion in stock, and Jensen Huang has sold more than $1 billion of his Nvidia shares. Yet every payday, the same automated order executes: millions of ordinary workers' pay automatically buys the overpriced AI stock that the billionaires are selling.
