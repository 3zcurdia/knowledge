# Historical Parallels — Railway Mania and Dark Fiber

## Britain's railway mania (1840s)

In the 1840s Britain got swept up in railway mania: building railway tracks soaked up about 7% of the economy, the equivalent of $2 trillion a year in today's money. The man taking their money was George Hudson, dubbed the railway king — at one point he controlled over a quarter of England's rail lines and used his seat in Parliament to make sure creditors could not arrest him. He ran his empire like a pyramid scheme, paying shareholders fat dividends out of their invested capital and dressing it up like profit; the payouts lasted only while fresh cash poured in. Hudson's scheme eventually came crashing down and railway stocks across Britain lost two thirds of their value, a third of the approved lines were never built, and families on modest incomes who had sunk their entire savings into railway shares lost everything. Once he lost his seat in Parliament, Hudson fled the country and was eventually caught and imprisoned.

## The dark fiber bubble (late 1990s)

In the late 1990s telecom giant WorldCom fed Wall Street a wildly inflated story about internet growth. The industry swallowed it whole and borrowed close to a trillion dollars to bury fiber optic cable from coast to coast — as much as 95% of it sat dark, just like the data centers in Ashburn. Global Crossing founder Gary Winnick saw the writing on the wall and, before the company imploded under $12.4 billion in debt, cashed out more than $700 million in stock. His workers never had that option: in Rochester, New York, 401(k) contributions came in company stock and employees were banned from selling it for five years. WorldCom crashed even harder, triggering the largest bankruptcy America had ever seen — its executives had disguised billions in everyday costs as long-term investments to prop up profits, the exact same accounting trick Michael Burry accuses the AI companies of. Inside WorldCom, employees watched $642 million in retirement savings shrink to less than $19 million; a federal judge sent CEO Bernie Ebbers to prison for 25 years, half a million telecom workers lost their jobs, and investors lost trillions.

## The one difference that matters

Dark fiber could sit in the ground for years and still carry the internet one day. The chips rotting inside empty data centers cannot wait that long: with every new chip launch, Nvidia wipes out billions in silicon.
